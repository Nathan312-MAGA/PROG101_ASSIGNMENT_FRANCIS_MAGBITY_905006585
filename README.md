##People Affected
•	Household residents: Live near dumping sites and suffer from foul odour, mosquitoes, rats and flies
•	Children: Play near waste piles and easily contract cholera, typhoid, malaria and skin infections
•	Women: Mostly responsible for cleaning homes and caring for sick family members
•	Elderly people and people with weak immunity: More vulnerable to diseases caused by pollution
•	Market traders and shop owners: Lose customers because of dirty surroundings and bad smell
•	Fishermen: Livelihood is destroyed when waste pollutes rivers and the sea
•	Farmers: Soil and water are contaminated by leachate from waste
•	Waste collectors and scavengers: Handle waste without protective gear and are exposed to sharp objects, toxic chemicals and infections
•	School pupils: Miss classes due to sickness caused by poor sanitation and schools surrounded by waste
•	Health workers: Face increased pressure from outbreaks of diseases caused by poor waste management
•	Drivers and pedestrians: Face blocked roads and drainages caused by waste
•	Flooding victims: Homes are destroyed when blocked drainages cause flooding during the rainy season
•	The whole community: Suffers from loss of beauty, reduced tourism and business opportunities and poor quality of life
##PROPOSED SOLUTION
A solution to community waste management in Freetown involves:
•	Community-led initiatives: Form waste committees for local planning and monitoring
•	Segregation at source: Households separate plastic, organic, paper, metal and general waste
•	Door-to-door collection: Use tricycles (Keke) for narrow streets and trucks for bulk waste
•	Community collection points: Designate spots for waste drop-off and sorting
•	Recycling and composting: Establish centers for processing recyclables and organic waste
•	Awareness campaigns: Educate on health risks of poor disposal and benefits of proper management
•	Partnerships: Collaborate with Freetown City Council, NGOs, private companies and communities
•	Fee-based system: Charge households a small monthly fee for sustainable operations
•	Train waste workers: Provide protective gear and skills for collection, sorting and recycling
•	Improve infrastructure: Build transfer stations, increase landfill capacity and provide bins
•	Enforce laws: Penalize indiscriminate dumping and reward good waste practices
•	Create jobs: Employ youth in collection, sorting, recycling and compost production
•	Monitor and evaluate: Track progress and adjust strategies based on feedback.
